## 1. Purpose and audience

AI Sharc is the AI Shopping Health Audit Report Card. It audits one firm, for one shopping query, and tells that firm what Shopping Agents can and cannot read about it, what to fix, and in what order, so it can be included in, and recommended over others in, a shopping-agent recommendation.

Audience for this specification: Tim and anyone building, reviewing, or quality-checking the audit internally. It is not sent to firms, not published on aisharc.com, and not shared with implementers, partners, or platforms without Tim's explicit go-ahead. Firms receive only their own Preliminary and Final Report Cards. See section 12 for what may be exposed publicly.

## 2. Scope

- **One audit =** one firm, one service query in plain words, one Primary Business Category, one town and ZIP, one evidence date.
- **Trades in Template 1:** HVAC, Plumbing, Electrical, Roofing only. Other trades require a new crosswalk before they are audited.
- **Launch scope, decided 2 Nov 2026:** HVAC in all of Texas. Template 1 still covers four trades, but the first launch is HVAC only, statewide in Texas, one firm, one query, one town and ZIP per card, not Coppell only. Launch, build, sale, and marketing details beyond scope are still to be decided.
- **Services in the worked example:** AC installation and repair, as separate services. Installation and replacement are treated as the same service family; repair is separate.
- **What the audit does not do:** It does not make fixes (audit only), it does not guarantee inclusion or ranking, it does not book, call, or test-book without the firm's permission where a test is needed, and it does not contact a firm about the audit without Tim's explicit go-ahead. It does not accept a manual confirmation from the Client Company as a Pass (see section 4.1, revised 2 Nov 2026).

## 3. Inputs, fixed before any check

Record on every Audit Data row and on every card, and do not change them mid-audit:

- Firm, exact name as listed
- Primary Business Category, with platform source (for example Google, gcid:hvac_contractor)
- Query (service), in plain words, install and repair stated separately
- Town and ZIP searched (for example Coppell, TX 75019)
- checked_at, ISO 8601 with seconds and offset (for example 2026-11-18T17:13:00-06:00, America/Chicago)
- Vendor — the corporation that supplies the Shopping Agent (for example Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Perplexity, xAI, AI Sharc). Open text, not a closed list: new vendors will appear.
- Product — the actual tool or agent used (for example MUSE-SPARK, Meta AI, ChatGPT Work, ChatGPT Dots, Claude, Gemini, harness). Open text, not a closed list: new products will appear.
- Competitor set, where a rank or approximate ranking is shown (query, date, and firms included)

The harness writes Vendor "AI Sharc" and Product "harness" for its own scored audits. Audit data produced by a Shopping Agent carries that agent's Vendor and Product.

A re-check re-runs the same query, Primary Business Category, and town and ZIP, on a new date, using the saved Report Card as the original check.

Task text is the full sentence as sent to the Shopping Agent (for example 'Find an HVAC firm whose service area includes Houston, TX 77002 for AC repair and compare the firms you find.'). Service query is the service in plain words (for example 'AC repair'). The audit's Query field holds the task text (the full sentence); the service query is recorded separately on the Run sheet.

The three standard shopping tasks (wording decided by Tim 2026-11-21), shown for Houston, TX 77002:

| Task | Task text |
|---|---|
| 1 — combined (installation and repair) | Find an HVAC firm whose service area includes Houston, TX 77002 for AC installation and repair and compare the firms you find. |
| 2 — repair | Find an HVAC firm whose service area includes Houston, TX 77002 for AC repair and compare the firms you find. |
| 3 — installation and replacement | Find an HVAC firm whose service area includes Houston, TX 77002 for AC installation and replacement and compare the firms you find. |

The task names the service area, not the firm's location, because a firm located outside the ZIP may still serve it, and naming only the location narrows the pool the agent picks from. The earlier wording ('Find an HVAC firm in [town], TX [ZIP] for …') is kept on the runs made with it (Houston, 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19); those runs are not re-labelled. No shopping query had been run with the new wording when it was adopted (2026-11-21).

## 4. Evidence sources and honesty rules

Check, in preference order for verification: the firm's website and town service-area page; Google Business Profile; Meta place; Yelp; bbb.org; the applicable state licence register; independent review platforms.

For every Item, record a source URL and checked_at (ISO 8601 with seconds and offset, §13). A claim on the firm's own website alone is not independent verification for rating or licence Items. Where an Item's source is generic in sample data (for example the Coppell service page for every row), a live audit must record the Item-specific source (for example bbb.org for the BBB Ranking Suggestion, the licence register or footer for the licence Item).

Honesty rules, which are standards, not preferences:

- Never estimate or fill in a point that could not be read. Mark it Not accessible. On cards, an Item that could not be automatically confirmed is shown as Not confirmed, and the original score stands. A firm's statement alone does not change it to Pass (see section 4.1, revised 2 Nov 2026).
- Not accessible means a gap in that review, not proof the firm lacks it today. State that on every card.
- Preserve conflicts as conflicts. Do not quote a single figure when sources disagree.
- One issue per row. A bundled fix-list row covering two Issues counts as two Issues.
- Single-section counting: an Issue is reported and counted in one section only, the section of its discrete Item. Its effect on another Category is covered in Why it matters for that Item, not as a second Issue.
- A Pass derived from a fix list means no Issue for that Item was recorded in the evidence used, not a fresh item-by-item audit. Say so on the card.
- Keep evidence depth separate: firms from thinner interim evidence are flagged, kept in a separate section, and require a full re-check before their grade is used with a client.

### 4.1 Access issues — what happens when data cannot be accessed (revised 2 Nov 2026, superseding the 1 Nov 2026 workflow)

When an Item cannot be accessed, the firm is told, in the Final Report Card, for each such Item: what was tried, the source that could not be read, and what it means (Not accessible / Not confirmed, not a proven absence). The firm has one route to a better score:

**Fix the access issue, so the auditor can re-check it automatically.** The Final card gives a specific suggestion for that source (see table below). Once access is fixed, the Item is re-scored as Observed Pass or Fail on re-check, within the re-check window covered by the price in section 12, at no extra charge.

**Manual confirmation is eliminated (decided 2 Nov 2026).** A confirmation submitted by the Client Company does not count, does not change a score, and is not shown as Firm-confirmed, because Firm-confirmed is no longer a status value. The Item stays shown as Not confirmed on the card, and the original score stands, until an automated re-check confirms it. Work continues on methods to perform automated confirms; no automated-confirm method is adopted yet, and no firm-supplied statement is a substitute for it.

#### 4.1.1 Website access — the two-method rule and the F condition (revised 16 Nov 2026, v2.0)

If the firm's website itself cannot be read because of a bot blocker or heavy JavaScript, the audit cannot verify the Items that depend on the website — exact name, services in plain words, area coverage, contact, and hours — so the firm fails the minimum condition in section 5 and the Grade is F, provided the reason is on the firm's side (see the Inability to Access Website Reason set below). The Final Report Card must tell the firm it needs to fix access: allow the audit crawler, publish a plain-text version of the service page, or reduce the JavaScript dependency so the service, area, phone, and hours are readable without executing heavy scripts. The audit records which method failed — the initial HTML returned without executing JavaScript, or the rendered page in a browser — and reports it. Two methods are tested in order: the initial HTML returned without executing JavaScript, then the rendered page in a browser. If the initial HTML fails but the rendered page reads, the website is readable — the minimum condition does NOT fail, the Items are verified from the rendered page, and the Key facts readable without JavaScript Item (M2, Category 7) fails, because the facts are not present in the initial HTML. The website is unreadable only when BOTH methods fail — the initial HTML fails AND the rendered page fails. This is an AI Sharc access failure because the audit cannot read a site it cannot verify; it is not a measured claim that a particular Shopping Agent cannot read the site.

Distinction, confirmed by Tim 2 Nov 2026: this access failure applies only when the company website itself is unreadable. If the website is readable but a single external source is blocked or unreadable (for example bbb.org, a Meta place, or a review platform), that Item alone fails as Not confirmed, and the original score stands — unless that source is the only way to satisfy an Item: if no verifiable listing outside the website can be found at all, the G6 verifiable listing Item itself fails (see section 4.3, rule 2). The AI crawler access Item (section 6.9, Category 7) is different from this rule: it fails when robots.txt blocks the crawlers Shopping Agents use to fetch answers, even though the website itself is readable — and it fails that Item (0 of its 3 points), not the whole Grade. A site that blocks every crawler, including the audit, fails under this section instead. A confirmed block of one Shopping Agent affects that agent's per-agent observation — that agent could not access the site — and the aggregate grade only through the AI crawler access Item (M1, Category 7). ### 4.2 Do-not-harvest check — delivery only, not scored (added 3 Nov 2026)

This check is about whether AI Sharc may send a Preliminary advertising email to a firm, not about how well the firm performs for shoppers. It is recorded once per audit, in a separate Delivery Check record, not as an Item or Ranking Suggestion, and it never changes a Grade, a Category score, or a High Priority count. A firm is never penalised for protecting its email address.

What to check, in order, and record with source URL and date for each:

1. **Published business email:** Is a business contact email published on the firm's website or listing? Record Yes or No, and the page where it was found. Do not record a personal employee email that was not published as a business contact, and do not invent an address.
2. **Collection method:** How was the address obtained — manual reading of a published contact page, or automated extraction? Automated extraction at scale is the method CAN-SPAM's harvesting provision is concerned with, so record the method honestly.
3. **Do-not-harvest notice:** Search the website footer, Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Contact page, and any anti-spam policy page for a notice that the site will not give, sell, or transfer addresses, or that prohibits harvesting, mining, or sending unsolicited commercial email. Record the exact wording quoted, the page URL, and the date. Record No notice found if none is found, not an assumption that none exists.
4. **Notice type, and what it means:** Distinguish two different notices, because they do not have the same effect:
   - A CAN-SPAM-style notice that the site will not give, sell, or transfer addresses maintained by the site: this is about the site operator giving addresses to others. On its own, it does not automatically ban a single manual email to a published business contact, but it is a warning against automated harvesting from that site.
   - A site term or policy that prohibits harvesting, or prohibits sending unsolicited commercial email to addresses from that site: treat this as a bar on sending an advertising email to an address obtained from that site.
   - No notice found: this does not by itself make sending lawful. The email must still meet CAN-SPAM (truthful headers and subject, identification as an advertisement, valid physical postal address, working opt-out honoured within 10 business days).

Decision rule for Preliminary delivery:

- **Advertising email may be sent** only if a published business email was found, no prohibiting notice was found, and the email will meet CAN-SPAM as above.
- **Advertising email must NOT be sent** if a prohibiting notice was found, if no published business email was found, if the address was obtained only by automated harvesting from a site with a do-not-harvest notice, or if CAN-SPAM requirements (including a valid postal address and opt-out) cannot be met.
- **If advertising email must not be sent, the website verification route remains available:** the firm can view its Preliminary Report Card on the website by entering and verifying its email address. That route is initiated by the firm, not by an outbound advertising email, and it does not require AI Sharc to email the firm first. Record which delivery route was used, and why, in the Delivery Check record.
- **Primary email address (defined 3 Nov 2026 — no Primary email address had been defined before this date).** The earlier definitions were Unique primary phone number and Primary Business Category; the email choice was explicitly open in Conflict C-07. Primary email address is now defined, on the same pattern as the unique primary phone, as the single business contact email for that location, decided in this order, with no discretion:
  1. If a business contact email is published on the firm's website Contact page as the main contact AND appears identically on at least one listing (Google, Meta, or Yelp), that email is the Primary email address.
  2. Else, if the website Contact page publishes exactly one business contact email, that email is the Primary email address, even if listings are silent or differ.
  3. Else, if no Contact page email exists and the website footer publishes exactly one business contact email, that email is the Primary email address.
  4. Else, no Primary email address exists for that firm in this audit. Record Multiple or None, do not choose arbitrarily, and do not send an advertising email; use the website verification route only.
  Record the Primary email address with its source URL and date, per audit, per location. If the Primary email changes on a re-check, the new audit records the new Primary; a change of email does not reset the Preliminary limit below.
- **Per-firm/per-query free Preliminary limit: 10 (set 3 Nov 2026).** For each firm, for each query (service, town, and ZIP — one firm, one query, one town and ZIP per card), AI Sharc may deliver at most 10 free Preliminary Report Cards in total. Count each distinct Preliminary card actually delivered for that firm and query, whether by advertising email or by a website verification view that delivers a card. Re-viewing an already-delivered Preliminary does not count as a new delivery. After 10 deliveries for that firm and query, no further free Preliminary for that firm and query will be sent or shown as a new free delivery. This limit is a maximum, not a permission: the do-not-harvest check, CAN-SPAM requirements, and any opt-out still override it, and a delivery that the check bars is not sent even if the count is below 10. This limit does not apply to Final Report Card re-checks, which remain unlimited within 60 days of the original Report Card (see section 12), and it does not reset on a new date unless Tim decides otherwise later. The Delivery Check record must log the count (1 to 10), date, route, and Primary email used for each delivery, and record 'Limit reached' for any further request.
- **The Delivery Check record is internal only.** It is not shown on a Preliminary or Final Report Card, and it does not appear as a failed Item or a fix for the firm to make. The firm is not asked to remove a do-not-harvest notice.

### 4.3 Hard rules — definitive definitions, limits, and what counts as a Conflict (adopted 3 Nov 2026)

These rules exist to minimise the number of conflicts. Where a Field Mapping row and this section appear to differ, this section governs, and the Field Mapping must be corrected to match, not the other way round.

**1. No auditor discretion on Pass/Fail.** If the evidence meets the Pass criteria exactly as written in the Field Mapping for that Item, record Pass. If it does not meet them, record Fail (Missing, Conflict, or Not accessible, as defined below). There is no 'close enough', no partial credit, no provisional Pass, and no averaging of figures from different sources. A passed Item earns its full points; a failed Item earns 0.

**2. Evidence required for every decision.** Every Item and Ranking Suggestion decision must cite a source URL, checked_at, and the exact wording or figure found. No source means Not accessible (for an Item) or Not checked (for a Ranking Suggestion), never Pass, and never Fail by inference — except where an Item expressly requires a thing to exist: the G6 verifiable listing Item, with no listing found after checking Google, Meta, and Yelp, is Fail, because the Item requires a listing outside the firm's own website.

**3. One failure, one count.** One failed Item is one High Priority issue, however many places the failure appears. Its effect on another Category is covered in Why it matters for that Item, not as a second Issue (single-section counting, already adopted). There is no Critical-issue count: F is a minimum condition (readable website, in business — section 5), not a count of failed checks.

**4. What counts as a Conflict — narrow definition.** A Conflict exists ONLY when one of these is true:
   (a) The same platform, the same scope, and the same date base are quoted differently in two places (for example, the same Google listing's rating quoted differently on the website and on the listing itself).
   (b) Two live adopted documents contradict each other on the same Item, Ranking Suggestion, or Grade definition.
   (c) A required field is present but its meaning cannot be determined from its own wording, and no other source resolves it, and the Item cannot honestly be marked Pass, Missing, or Not accessible under the definitions below. Then mark Conflict, do not guess.
   If none of (a)-(c) is true, it is not a Conflict, whatever else it is. Mark it Pass, Pass with warning, Missing, or Not accessible, as its own criteria require.

**5. What is NOT a Conflict.** The following are never Conflicts, and must not be recorded as Conflicts or used to create a second Issue:
   - Figures that differ across platforms (Google vs Yelp vs a firm's own site). Platforms have different populations, volumes, solicitation, filtering, and time windows, so cross-platform figures are expected to differ. Report each figure per platform, with platform, count, and date. Never average them, and never quote a figure without its platform, scope, and date base.
   - Category names that differ across platforms (for example, Google 'HVAC Contractor' vs a Meta category name vs a Yelp alias). Record the Primary Business Category per platform; a different name on another platform is not a conflict.
   - NAICS codes that differ by version (2017 vs 2022). Record the version used; a version difference is not a conflict.
   - The same Issue appearing in more than one Category. Count it once, in the section of its discrete Item.
   - A source being silent. Silence is not a contradiction. A founding year stated on one source only, with other sources silent, is not a Conflict (see Pass with warning, rule 6). A rating readable on one platform only, with other platforms Not accessible, is not a Conflict.
   - A rebrand or rename without a mapping page. That is Fail for the founding-year Item and for the G1 exact name Item, not a Conflict, unless the same source claims both the old and the new name as current at the same time.
   - A licence or BBB figure that cannot be read on the register or on bbb.org. That is Not accessible, shown as Not confirmed, not a Conflict, unless the register itself shows two different numbers for the same firm.

**6. Pass with warning — a Pass, not a Fail.** Pass with warning is a new Item status value (see section 7). It counts as Pass for the Item's points and for the Grade — the Item earns its full points — it does NOT count as a High Priority issue, and it is shown on the Final card in Details with its warning note, and on the Preliminary card as Pass with a warning note in the recap. Use it ONLY in these cases, where the Pass criteria are met but a material caveat a shopper should know is present:
   - Reputation rating: the rating is readable with platform, count, and date, but figures differ across platforms. Warning: 'Ratings differ by platform, as expected; figures are reported per platform, not averaged.'
   - Reputation rating: the rating is readable on one platform only, and other platforms are Not accessible. Warning: 'Rating read on [platform] only; other platforms Not accessible in this audit.'
   - Founding year: a single founding year is stated on one source, other sources are silent, and no lineage contradiction exists. Warning: 'Founding year stated on [source] only; other sources silent.'
   - Hours: day-by-day hours are readable, and a 24-hour or emergency claim on another source is also verifiable and does not contradict a stated day. Warning: 'Hours as stated, plus a verifiable [24-hour/emergency] claim on [source].'
   - Any other case that meets the Pass criteria but carries a caveat that does not meet any Fail criteria: record the caveat verbatim, and do not upgrade it to a Fail or a Conflict. Pass with warning must never be used to soften a genuine Fail, and never be used where the Pass criteria are not met. If the Pass criteria are not met, the Item is Fail, not Pass with warning.

**7. Items — hard Pass/Fail.** For each Item, Pass requires ALL of its Field Mapping Pass criteria, with evidence for each. Fail if ANY criterion is missing or contradicted, as follows (evidence for each criterion must be recorded):
   - G1 exact name: Fail if the name differs on any checked source and no old-to-new mapping page explains it. Name comparison uses the normalisation in section 6.6: 'A/C' compares equal to 'AC', and case, punctuation, and trailing legal suffixes are ignored; a name that differs by a real word still differs.
   - C2.2 install and repair listed separately on that page: Fail if installation/replacement OR repair is missing from the service page, or lumped together without separate plain-words wording. The check is service-page only (decided 2026-11-16); listings are not required.
   - C2.1 service-area page naming town and ZIP: Fail if the town and ZIP are not explicitly named on the service-area page.
   - C6.1 one unique primary phone number everywhere: Fail if more than one phone number is used for the location across sources, or if no unique primary phone can be identified. Multiple numbers are Fail, not Conflict, unless the same source shows two numbers for the same location with no explanation.
   - C2.4 unique primary phone and booking route on that page: Fail if no working booking, quote, or contact route exists on that page.
   - C5.1 day-by-day hours readable: Fail if no readable day-by-day hours and no verifiable 24-hour or emergency claim exist. (A closed flag is not an Item fail — a firm that is not in business is F under section 5.)
   - G6 verifiable listing: Fail if no listing outside the firm's own website is found after checking Google, Meta, and Yelp, or if the listing found does not match name, phone, service area, or Primary Business Category.
   An Item that is Not accessible because the website itself is unreadable is not scored — the firm is F under section 5. An Item whose external source alone is blocked, while the website is readable, is Not confirmed for that Item only.

**8. Ranking Suggestions — hard statuses.** Ranking Suggestions are never scored, never affect a Category score, a total, a Grade, or an Issue count, whatever their status. The statuses are decided only as follows. The six Section 6 factors use Observed, Recommendation, and Not checked:
   - Observed: public evidence on the website or listing directly proves the ranking suggestion, with URL and date, and no live test is needed. Per ranking suggestion: S1 Booking and quote route is Observed only if the website shows a booking or quote route AND what happens next AND that a quote is given in writing before work; a form alone, with no next step and no written-quote statement, is Recommendation, not Observed. S2 Availability is Observed only if a next available appointment or emergency response window is published on the website, or a disclosed test with the firm's permission proves it; never estimate availability from the website. S3 Price for this user is Observed only if a diagnostic fee in dollars and a price range or written-quote process are published; a fee alone, with no price or quote process, is Recommendation. S4 Brand and system coverage is Observed if the brands and system types actually served are listed on the service page. S5 Service-area dispatch is Observed only from a disclosed test or firm evidence proving dispatch reality; a service-area page alone is not dispatch Observed, it is Recommendation. S6 Machine discovery (llms.txt) is Observed only if /llms.txt exists, is reachable, and lists the firm's services and service area in plain words; otherwise it is Recommendation.
   - Recommendation: the ranking suggestion cannot be observed, but a specific suggestion exists in the Final card. It stays a Recommendation, with the original score unchanged, until it can be observed automatically.
   - Not checked: the ranking suggestion was not tested and no public evidence for it was checked. Not checked is not a deficiency, not a Fail, and must not be presented as one.
   The two Section 4 Ranking Suggestions use Complete, Suggested, and Not checked (decided by Tim 2026-11-18):
   - Complete: the firm already has it. C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org is Complete only if the firm is BBB accredited AND its rating on bbb.org is consistent with the rating shown on the firm's site or listings, with the bbb.org profile read and the URL and date recorded. C4.1 six-line warranty card is Complete only if the six-line warranty card is present on the site, with all six lines and real numbers (see §6.7), with the URL and date recorded.
   - Suggested: the firm does not have it — the suggestion is made to the firm in Section 4 of the Final card. C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org is Suggested when the firm is not BBB accredited, or its rating on bbb.org does not match the rating shown on the firm's site or listings, or no BBB accreditation or rating is published. C4.1 six-line warranty card is Suggested when the six-line warranty card is not published, or is published without all six lines and real numbers.
   - Not checked: the check itself cannot be performed. C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org is Not checked when bbb.org cannot be read for the firm (for example bbb.org unreachable, or no BBB profile found on bbb.org); C4.1 six-line warranty card is Not checked when the site cannot be read for the warranty card. Not checked is not a deficiency, not a Fail, and must not be presented as one.
   A firm's statement alone is never Observed for any ranking suggestion that needs a live test, and never Complete for C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org or C4.1 six-line warranty card — Complete requires the public evidence above.

**9. Previous results are ignored; conflicts must be based on current templates and cards.** The 25 Oct 2026 audit results, the Top 10 ranking, the 19-firm fix lists, and any audit conflict derived from them are historical evidence only. They are not current evidence, not a current Grade for live use, and not a current conflict. They must not be used to score a firm, pitch a firm, or close a current-template conflict. The Airtron Preliminary and Final cards remain as format examples only, with their 25 Oct 2026 evidence date, and are not a current audit of Airtron. A conflict is Open only if it arises from the current templates and cards (Specification v2.6, Field Mapping Rev 3 (2026-11-19), and the current card revisions) or from a fresh audit under those templates, with a new evidence date. A historical audit conflict is closed as Superseded, and is re-raised only if a fresh audit under current templates reproduces it, with new evidence. Historical files are retained, not deleted, and historical totals (82 High Priority issues, 101 total with 19 Critical, for 25 Oct 2026 evidence) remain as historical totals for that evidence date only.

**10. Limits that prevent cross-card conflicts.** One firm, one query, one town and ZIP per card. A difference across towns, queries, or evidence dates is not a Conflict, it is a different card. A rating figure without platform, count, and date is Not confirmed, not a Conflict. A founding year must be one year, with lineage explained through any rebrand or rename, and an old-to-new mapping page if rebranded; there is no 'circa' tolerance and no range. A licence check must name one number, one named holder, and one register; a register figure that differs from a website claim is Fail for the licence Item if the register is decisive, or Not confirmed if the register cannot be read, not a Conflict, unless the register itself contradicts itself.

**11. Company fields — validated on every run (Field Mapping Company sheet).** The agent-facing Field Mapping's Company sheet records 11 fields for the firm on every run: firm name (as named by the agent), position in the agent's comparison, Primary Business Category, website URL, primary phone, address / town + ZIP, rating (review rating), review count, services (install and repair, and others), hours, and Do Not Harvest — notice status (No / Yes). ## 5. Minimum condition — Website Access (F)

There is no Stage 1 of Critical Gates. The audit has one minimum condition, checked before any Item is scored: the firm's website must be readable, and the firm must be in business. A firm that fails the minimum condition is Grade F. Everything else is graded on the 100-point total in section 8.

**F if any of these is true:**

- **The website is unreachable on the firm's side.** The Inability to Access Website Reason (section 4.1.1) is CERT-INVALID (TLS certificate expired or invalid), PARKED (domain serves a parking / domain-for-sale page), DEAD (domain does not resolve or server does not respond), SUSPENDED (hosting account suspended), or WRONG-CONTENT (URL returns a different business, or a generic lead-generation page with no firm identity). A firm with **no website at all** is also F — it provides no site to read. A Shopping Agent may still include such a firm in its answer from a listing, but the shopping data a recommendation needs — price, warranty, hours, services, and booking — is difficult to determine without a website, so the firm cannot be graded on it.
- **The firm is not in business.** The closed flag: the website or listing indicates the firm is closed / permanently closed. This is the Active Status element of the former G5 hours / Active Status Gate (decided by Tim 2026-11-15: a firm that is not in business goes into the F group).

Exception — Facebook page: if the Shopping Agents read the firm's Facebook page when there is no website, the firm is not automatically F. The Facebook page is then the firm's readable presence for the audit. Which Items can be scored from a Facebook page, and what counts as the Shopping Agents reading it, is an open decision (see §16).

Before a firm is declared to have no website, the auditor must complete the minimum discovery set: (a) four to five search queries (name + town; name + 'website'; name + phone; name + services; name + town + directory); (b) a Places details check for a website link; (c) a Facebook page / Yelp profile website-link check; (d) directory listings for a website link; (e) a live-fetch verification of any candidate domain. Only if the full set finds no own website is the firm recorded as having no website. The audit then records the Inability to Access Website Reason NO-WEBSITE (firm side, section 4.1.1); the firm is F under this section, subject to the section 16 Facebook-page exception, which is an open decision.

**Not F:**

- A reason on AI Sharc's side (APPROVAL-EXPIRED, PROXY-REFUSED) means the audit is unverified, not F. No Grade is assigned until a re-check verifies the site.
- BLOCKED and JS-ONLY are decided by the rendered read under the two-method rule (section 4.1.1).
- **"Not taking on new customers" is not an F condition** (decided by Tim 2026-11-15). A firm can be open but not accepting new clients; a Shopping Agent would most likely still include such a firm, since it does not check capacity and there is no structured "accepting new customers" field in the listings it reads. It may be treated as a flag on the firm's card in future; it is not an F condition.

**For an F firm:** no Items are scored, no points are given, and the firm is not ranked — it is not in the eligible set (section 8). The card states the reason in Section 2 Website Access (section 11) and tells the firm what to fix so a re-check can grade it. The former six Gates are merged into the Items in section 6: G2 services in plain words → C2.2 install and repair listed separately on that page; G3 area coverage → C2.1 service-area page naming town and ZIP; G4 contact → C6.1 one unique primary phone number everywhere (phone part) and C2.4 unique primary phone and booking route on that page (booking-route part); G5 hours / Active Status → C5.1 day-by-day hours readable (hours part) and the F condition above (Active Status part); G1 exact name and G6 verifiable listing become scored Items. There is no Critical-issue count.

## 7. Status values (controlled vocabulary)

Use only these values. Do not invent others in Audit Data or on cards.

- Item: Pass, Pass with warning, Missing, Conflict, Not accessible. On cards, a Not accessible Item is displayed as Fail — Not confirmed, and the original score stands. Pass with warning counts as Pass — the Item earns its full points — does not count as a High Priority issue, and is shown with its warning note (see section 4.3, rule 6). Firm-confirmed is no longer a status value.
- Item points: a passed Item earns its full points; a failed Item earns 0. No partial credit.
- Category result: points earned out of points available (for example 13 of 22). A Category result is a points subtotal, not a Pass/Fail.
- Ranking suggestion: the six Section 6 factors (S1 Booking and quote route, S2 Availability, S3 Price for this user, S4 Brand and system coverage, S5 Service-area dispatch, S6 Machine discovery (llms.txt)) use Observed, Recommendation, Not checked. The two Section 4 Ranking Suggestions (C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org and C4.1 six-line warranty card) use Complete, Suggested, Not checked (decided by Tim 2026-11-18).

Category result rows are totals, not Items. They are never counted as passed or failed Items, and never counted as High Priority issues.

F is not a status value. F is the Grade for a firm that fails the minimum condition in section 5 (website unreachable on the firm's side, or not in business).

### 7.1 Observation reason codes

A Shopping Agent observation row recorded as Not observed carries one reason code, plus a short explanation in the Reason field.

(a) Not mentioned. The agent's answer, including its sources panel and any follow-up turns, does not state the fact and does not show that the agent checked the Item.

(b) Mentioned, not demonstrated. The answer states the fact, but either (1) there is no recordable evidence for it in the response body, sources panel, or follow-up turn — no exact agent wording with a cited source to record — or (2) what it states does not meet the Item's pass criteria in full (for example, hours given as one daily range rather than day by day, or one firm's figure where the Item requires consistency across sources).

Sources-panel rule: content in the agent's sources panel is part of the response and can support a Pass. A sources-panel snippet with a cited source can support a Pass for the Item it evidences. Record where the evidence came from: response body, sources panel, or follow-up turn.

Neither code is a Fail. Both are excluded from the cross-agent majority (§8.1). The codes apply to observation rows only, never to official audit rows, which use the Item statuses in §7.

Status mapping for a directed AI Sharc Audit (decided by Tim 2026-11-19). In a directed AI Sharc Audit, agents use the official §7 statuses (Pass, Pass with warning, Missing, Conflict, Not accessible) plus Not completed when a tool was unavailable. Not observed and reason codes (a)/(b) stay for shopping-query observations only. ### 8.1 Per-agent observations and cross-agent summary (aggregation) (added 10 Nov 2026, at Tim's instruction)

AI Sharc assesses a business for discovery and comparison across major buy-side personal agents (Shopping Agents), retains each agent's findings, and aggregates them into actionable recommendations. Three distinct outputs are preserved:

- **AI Sharc grade** — compliance with the fixed audit criteria in this Specification (the 21 scored Items, Category subtotals, and grade bands). The grade is determined only by the AI Sharc Audit; per-agent observations never change the grade.
- **Per-agent observations** — what each tested agent actually found, read, verified, or could not access, recorded per agent, date, query, and location in the Agent Validation record (Field Mapping, Agent Validation sheet). An agent that has not been tested is Not tested, never a failure. A confirmed block of one agent affects that agent's per-agent observation only — that agent could not access the site — and the aggregate grade only through the AI crawler access Item (M1, Category 7).
- **Cross-agent summary** — common deficiencies, agent-specific problems, and prioritized fixes, showing how many agents were tested. The cross-agent result for each Item is decided by a majority rule across the tested agents that completed the check for that Item: **Pass if more than half of them pass that Item; Fail if half or fewer pass.** A common deficiency is an Item that fails the majority rule. An agent-specific problem is an Item that fails for one agent but passes the majority rule. Fixes are prioritized by how many tested agents found the issue, then by AI Sharc grade impact. The cross-agent summary always states how many agents were tested. This majority rule leaves room to add more agents — the threshold scales with the tested set.

The tested set starts with the agents Tim named on 10 Nov 2026 (for example Muse, ChatGPT Work (OAI-WORK), Dots (OAI-DOTS), Gemini Spark); more agents are added as they are tested. Agent names are recorded in the Agent Validation sheet only when verified against the vendor's own pages or a dated test.

Rules:
- Untested agents remain Not tested, rather than counting as failures. Only tested agents count in the majority.
- Only completed checks count in a majority. If an agent did not complete the check for an Item — for example, the check was interrupted, or a tool it needed was unavailable — that agent is excluded from that Item's majority; it is not counted as a Pass or a Fail for that Item. The cross-agent summary states how many agents completed the check for each Item.
- A delegated result adds no independent vote. When one agent's result for an Item came by delegating to another agent, the per-agent observations record the delegation, and only the originator's result counts in that Item's majority. For example, if a Dots run delegates a check to ChatGPT Work, the Work result counts once; the Dots observation records that it came via Work.
- An agent omitting a fact from its answer does not prove it could not read it. If an agent's answer does not mention a fact, that Item is recorded as Not observed for that agent, not as a Fail — a Fail requires the agent to have checked and found the fact absent or wrong.
- The majority rule is strict: with two tested agents that both completed the check, one Pass and one Fail is a Fail, because half or fewer passed.
- Different agents' recommendation positions are not averaged. No average, no combined rank, without a defined method.
- When one agent delegates to another (for example, a dot starts or manages a task in ChatGPT Work), the per-agent observations record the delegation, so the cross-agent summary does not imply independent corroboration.
- A Shopping Agent, for AI Sharc's purposes, is an AI agent that helps a user discover, compare, and select products or services. The ability to complete a purchase is NOT a requirement to be classified as a Shopping Agent in AI Sharc's application (see Definition of Terms, 'Shopping agent'). OpenAI Work and Dots are classified by experience, not as shopping agents (answered 11 Nov 2026): two separately labeled experience audits — AI Sharc — OpenAI ChatGPT Work Audit (internal ID OAI-WORK) and AI Sharc — OpenAI Dots Audit (internal ID OAI-DOTS) — identify the experience tested, without claiming either is a dedicated shopping product. For each audit run, record the model, tools, date, query, and location separately. If Dots delegates to Work, preserve that fact; the results are not independent corroboration. Both audits stay Not tested until their first documented runs.
- Firms named in a follow-up turn are in scope for Stage 2, labelled as follow-up (decided by Tim 2026-11-18).

## 13. Audit Data schema

One row per Category result, per Item, and per ranking suggestion. There are no Gate rows. The do-not-harvest check is NOT a row in Audit Data. It is a separate one-record-per-audit Delivery Check (see section 4.2), so reconciliation counts — Category result rows = 7, Item rows = 21, ranking suggestion rows = 8 — are unchanged by it. Audit rows per audit: 7 + 21 + 8 = 36. The eight ranking suggestion rows are the six Section 6 factors plus C3.2 BBB accreditation and rating consistent with bbb.org and C4.1 six-line warranty card, which are carried as un-scored Ranking Suggestion rows (decided 2026-11-18, Tim). New audits under v2.6 record spec_version '2.6' with 36 rows (0/7/21/8). Under v2.0 the count was 34 rows (0/7/21/6). Under v1.22 the count was 40 (6 Gate rows + 7 + 21 + 6); the Gate rows are gone — G1 exact name and G6 verifiable listing are Item rows, and the absorbed Gates have no rows. Columns, in order, as in the sample file: #; Firm; Primary Business Category; Query (service); Town + ZIP searched; checked_at; Stage; Row type (Category result, Item, Ranking suggestion); Report Card Category / Gate; Item; Item result; Source URL; Evidence note; Why it matters; Website change; Listing change; Operational change; Fix priority; Counts toward; Populates.

The audit-data.json run block records Vendor and Product alongside run_id, firm, query, town and ZIP, checked_at, and spec_version. It also records primary_email (the Primary email address as defined in section 4.2, or None when no Primary email exists for the firm in that audit) and primary_email_source (the source URL where it was found, with date). The Primary email address is a per-audit attribute of the run record, not a per-row audit column. Vendor and Product are required and non-empty on every v2.6 audit; the loader rejects a v2.6 file that omits them. They identify which Shopping Agent gathered the audit data; they do not affect any score, grade, Category score, or Item result.

An audit is identified by firm, query, town/ZIP, checked-at timestamp, vendor and product. checked_at is the single identity field for the audit's date and time: ISO 8601 with seconds and offset (for example 2026-11-18T17:13:00-06:00, America/Chicago), and the filename timestamp is its compact form. time_checked and timezone are dropped as separate fields from the run record; their information is inside checked_at's offset. A same-day re-run of the same firm, query, and town/ZIP is a distinct audit, identified by its time; parallel audits of the same firm by different agents are distinct audits, identified by vendor and product.

### File naming and folder layout

**Query ID.** query_id = <trade>-<town>-<zip>-t<task>, all lowercase, where trade is the trade name (hvac, plumbing, electrical, roofing); town is the town name with spaces and punctuation removed; zip is the 5-digit ZIP; and task is 1, 2 or 3. Examples: hvac-houston-77002-t2, hvac-coppell-75019-t1, hvac-wichitafalls-76301-t3.

**Per-firm audit files.** Each firm's audit is stored under runs/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<firm_id>/, named <firm_id>__<query_id>__<vendor>-<product>__<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS±hhmm>__audit-data.json. Worked example: runs/2026-11-18/24sevenac/24sevenac__hvac-houston-77002-t2__aisharc-harness__20261118T171300-0600__audit-data.json. The timestamp keeps the ISO 'T' separator as in the example; only the query_id is all lowercase — do not lowercase the 'T'.

**The four files per audit.** The other three files for the same audit share the same prefix, with the suffixes __summary.json, __human-review.json, and __delivery-check.json (for example 24sevenac__hvac-houston-77002-t2__aisharc-harness__20261118T171300-0600__summary.json).

**Rules.** All names are lowercase, with no spaces, and double-underscore separators, so a filename is script-splittable on '__'. The filename timestamp carries seconds and the UTC offset, and must equal the file's checked_at; the loader rejects a file whose filename timestamp does not match its checked_at. The database keeps the file's hash (sha256 of the file bytes), so a renamed copy of a file is recognised as already loaded.

**checked_at.** checked_at is a run-record field, ISO 8601 with seconds and offset (for example 2026-11-18T17:13:00-06:00, America/Chicago). The filename timestamp is the compact form of checked_at (20261118T171300-0600 for that example).

**Observation files.** For new runs, observation files use the double-underscore pattern: `<agent>__<query_id>__r<repeat>__<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS±hhmm>__shopping-query-return.xlsx` for the Shopping Query Return workbook and `<agent>__<query_id>__r<repeat>__<YYYYMMDDTHHMMSS±hhmm>__observation.md` for the observation file, where `<agent>` is the vendor-product pair (for example meta-metaai), `<query_id>` follows the query_id rule above, `<repeat>` is the repeat number (1 for the first run of a query on an agent, 2+ for fresh-session repeats), and the timestamp is the run's checked_at in compact form, with seconds and offset. Observation files are per-run, not per-firm, so the per-firm '__' audit-file pattern does not apply to them as firm files; they are named per run under this pattern. Existing observation files keep their current human-readable names, as used in the houston-2026-11-18 run folder: the Shopping Query Return workbook is named "AI Sharc — Shopping Query Return — <Town> <State> <ZIP> — Task <n> <service> — <YYYY-MM-DD> — Rev <n>.xlsx" (for example "AI Sharc — Shopping Query Return — Houston TX 77002 — Task 1 combined — 2026-11-18 — Rev 1.xlsx"), and the observation file is named "<VENDOR> — <Town> <State> <ZIP> — Task <n> <service> — observation — <YYYY-MM-DD>.md" (for example "META-AI — Houston TX 77002 — Task 1 combined — observation — 2026-11-18.md"). This is the naming the Observation Design §9.4 pointer refers to.

**Scope.** This convention applies to new audits from v2.5 onward. Existing audit files keep their current names: they were recorded without seconds and cannot be renamed honestly.

### Official audit — consolidation of the harness audit and agent audits

The harness audit is the base audit for a firm and query. The official audit for that firm and query combines the harness audit with every agent audit of the same firm, query and evidence date. For each Item, the official result is the majority across the audits that completed that Item; an audit recorded as Not accessible for that Item is excluded from that Item's majority. The harness audit breaks a tie. If any audit reached the site and returned data, the firm is not F or unverified for access on that evidence date, and those audits supply the evidence for the official audit. consolidated_from lists the audits used for the official audit (each by vendor, product, and checked_at). How an agent audit's status counts in the majority (pass or fail) follows the status mapping in §7.1 (decided by Tim 2026-11-19).

Boundary: this consolidation is about audits that reached the site. The no-website F rule is separate: a firm with no own website found after the minimum discovery set in §5 is still F (NO-WEBSITE, firm side), subject to the §16 Facebook-page exception, which is an open decision. Consolidation does not rescue a no-website firm, and a no-website firm is not consolidated into a graded official audit.

A firm is audited under each query whose run named it. A firm named in more than one task is audited under each of those tasks' queries, not only under the query of the task where it first appeared. Cards and rankings use each firm's latest complete official audit for that query (complete = website access resolved and reconciliation passes); see §8.

Derivation rules: Report Card Summary is derived from Audit Data, never typed separately. Grade from total points. Category score from Category result rows, verified against Item rows. High count = failed Items only. Eligible rank and percentile from the eligible set. Approximate ranking labelled with query, date, and competitor set. The Final card's Exact Fix is a single section; Audit Data's Website / Listing / Operational change columns are the source detail for that section, combined in prose on the card, not dropped.

Reconciliation checks, before any release: Audit Data rows total = 36; Category result rows = 7; Item rows = 21; ranking suggestion rows = 8; High issues = failed Items only; passed Items; Category points subtotals; Grade from total points. Reconciliation must match the cards, not just itself.

### Firm list and firm matching (added v2.10, 2026-11-21)

**The central firm list.** The firms table in the audit database is the central firm list: the firm universe for the AI Sharc Audit side. Shopping Agents are never sent it; they discover firms themselves. A firm an agent names that matches no firm in the list enters the list as agent-named, with its discovery source naming the run (§8.1, Overview §3.3). A firm in the list that no agent names is the invisibility signal. 

**Verified firm.** A firm is verified if it has a Meta place ID or an own website; either one is enough (decided by Tim 2026-11-21). A firm with neither is unverified. The central firm list holds verified firms; name-only firms are held out of the database until a Meta place ID or own website is found for them. The one exception is a firm an agent names: it enters the list even with neither, and is recorded as unverified until one is found. Each firm in the list carries its verification status, which is set to verified as soon as a Meta place ID or own website is recorded for it. Other verification sources can be added later only by a Specification change.

**Licence reference list.** The current Texas TDLR A/C Contractor licences are kept in the audit database as a separate reference list, not in the central firm list (Tim, 2026-11-21: kept for future reference, not deleted). Each record holds the business name as TDLR gives it (or the licence holder's name where TDLR gives none), the licence type, number, expiration date and county, and the licence holder. Only licences unexpired on the read date are kept (the TDLR dataset has no status column; §14). The reference list is used to find firms and licences, never to grade or rank: a licence record enters the central firm list only when a Meta place ID or own website is found for it, through the matching ladder. Where a licence record's name matches a firm in the central list, or another licence record, the match is kept with the reference list as a lead, not as a link, because rung 2 needs a town and ZIP that the TDLR dataset does not give for A/C Contractors. The reference list is in the test database only (Test AI Sharc Orchestrator).

**Matching ladder.** Two records are the same firm only when a rung matches, tried in this order:

1. the same Meta place ID;
2. the same licence type and licence number, and the same town and ZIP;
3. the same normalised website, and the same town and ZIP.

The normalised website is the website's host name, lowercase, without a leading www. A shared host that is not the firm's own site — a social network page (for example facebook.com), a manufacturer's site (for example carrier.com, trane.com) or a website builder's shared domain — is not a website for rung 3. A rung match between records whose names and phones both differ is queued for review, not merged, because different firms can share a site. A name alone never matches. A record that matches a firm by name but conflicts with it on phone, website or licence is not merged, updated or linked: it goes to the review queue. The ladder applies to merges inside the list, to loading records into the list, and to linking an agent mention (agent_run_firms) to a firm. An agent mention that matches a firm by name only stays unmatched and is queued for review.

**Merges.** When two records are the same firm, the record with a known town is canonical, both Meta place IDs are kept on it, and the sources merged are recorded. Branches at different locations with different phones are separate firms, not duplicates. A record with no town, no place ID and no phone that cannot be tied to one location goes to the review queue, not to an automatic merge.

**Loading into existing records: enrich, don't overwrite.** Loading list data into an existing firm record fills empty fields only. It never changes the firm's ID and never overwrites a value already recorded (licence, audit links, or anything else).

**Review queue.** Every open question the ladder cannot settle is a row in the review queue (firm_review_queue), with the reason, the rung tried and the conflicting values. An open item never changes a firm record or an agent mention by itself; it is resolved by a person.

**Uniqueness.** A firm's name is not unique: branches of one company share a name. The firm ID (a lowercase slug) is the key. The uniqueness rule is (normalised name, town and ZIP), with the name normalised as in §6.6; it is enforced in the database once the normalised name is populated. The column list is the Database fields sheet of the Licensed Home Trade Field Mapping (Rev 11).

### Approved automated confirmation methods (approved 3 Nov 2026)

Screen scraping and other automated methods are approved for confirming key data, as long as they meet public regulations and restrictions. Approved methods include: website crawling, Places and Yelp APIs, LLM extraction with a rules engine, screen scraping of publicly accessible pages, public register lookups (for example TSBPE and TDLR registers and bbb.org where readable), and structured-data parsing (for example schema.org where a firm publishes it). Approval is conditional on all of the following guardrails, which are hard rules, not guidance:
- Only publicly accessible, non-login data. No bypassing a login, paywall, CAPTCHA, bot blocker, or other technical access control. If a page is bot-blocked, do not scrape around it: the website-access rule in section 4.1.1 still applies, and the affected Item stays Not confirmed — or the firm is F if the website itself is unreadable — as that rule requires.
- Respect a site's published restrictions. If a site's Terms, robots.txt, or do-not-harvest notice prohibits scraping or harvesting for that data, do not scrape that site for that data; record Not confirmed and use the website verification route only for email delivery where applicable.
- Comply with CAN-SPAM for any email collection (see section 4.2), with copyright (record facts, source URL, and date; do not reproduce large copyrighted text or images on cards), and with privacy (only published business data; no personal employee data beyond a published business contact).
- Use ordinary request rates. Do not overload a site, and do not use a method that amounts to a denial-of-service or to bulk harvesting from a site with a do-not-harvest notice.
- Record the method for every confirmed Item: method (API, crawl, scrape, register lookup, structured data), source URL, date, and exact figure or wording. A scraped figure without source and date is Not confirmed, not Pass.
- Scraping does not change the status rules in sections 4.3 and 7. If scraped evidence meets the Pass criteria, record Pass; if it does not, record Fail or Not confirmed as those rules require; if scraping fails, the Item stays Not confirmed with the original score standing, until a successful automated re-check confirms it. A firm's statement alone is never a scraped confirmation, and manual confirmation remains eliminated.
- This approval does not authorise contacting a firm, sending an email the do-not-harvest check bars, or publishing a firm's data. Those remain governed by sections 4.2, 11, and 12.

## 18. How to keep this consistent going forward

1. Change this specification first. Log the change in section 17, with date, reason, and affected files.
2. Update the Definition of Terms second, but only for words. Do not add rules to the glossary.
3. Update sample audit data third, only as an example. Re-run reconciliation; it must match the cards.
4. Update the cards fourth, from the specification, not from memory. Check file names against titles plus revision date.
5. Update the skill fifth, as the internal playbook, so the next audit follows the same rules.
6. Before any release, run the banned-terms check: no Box, canonical phone, Muse as a product name, Readiness for the overall result, Shopping Snapshot as a separate field, separate Failed Critical Issues document, or Firm-confirmed as a status value; no 'Critical Gate' or 'Gate' as a live scoring stage (historical references in the version history, change log, Deviation Log, and dated card Rev notes are dated records, not live rules). Spelling standard, decided 2 Nov 2026: 'licence' is retained as is in AI Sharc documents; do not 'correct' it to 'license' in an automated spelling fix. Run the reconciliation checks in section 13. Run the internal-only check in section 12: no internal file, firm data, or grade is published or sent to another firm.
7. Keep one set of files, not copies. Move, do not copy, when a file moves folders, so there is no drifting duplicate. Review copies live in a dated review folder until approved, then they replace the live files, and the old live files are archived with a date suffix, not deleted immediately.
8. If a new instruction conflicts with this specification, follow section 15: flag it, log it, and wait for an explicit override or a specification change. Silence is not approval, and a one-time exception does not change the standard.
